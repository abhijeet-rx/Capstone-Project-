Operational Background

Sensors drift long before failure. Ordinary alarms sound only at breakdown.

Railway Air Production Units

Urban metro trains depend entirely on the compressor-driven Air Production Unit (APU) to supply pressurized air for mechanical braking, suspension balancing, and door actuation.

An abrupt compressor breakdown halts the train mid-track, strands passengers, and causes immediate network-wide delays.

Crucially, component wear does not happen in a millisecond: oil heats up, pressure dynamics degrade, and motor coils heat over dozens of hours. Conventional fixed tripwires ignore these early whispers until catastrophic trip limits are breached.

Conventional Tripwire vs Progressive Anomaly

The real question is not simply "can machine learning detect failure?", but: which workflow generates reliable warning lead time while keeping false-warning burden low enough for operational maintenance crews to trust?

Benchmark Evaluation

Good validation scores were a trap.

Standard supervised classifiers and complex neural architectures achieved strong validation metrics, but collapsed below the test prevalence baseline ($\sim 0.000611$) when evaluated on the prospective test period.

Test Average Precision ÷ Test Prevalence Chance baseline = 1.0× (dashed marker)
1.0× (Chance)
Key Finding: Isolation Forest scored the highest test AP ratio (4.75× prevalence, test ROC-AUC 0.8944), but produced zero qualifying persistent warnings at its evaluated threshold. Pure ranking capacity is not equivalent to an operational alarm system. Conversely, MLP achieved high validation AP (0.068948) yet fell to 0.53× on prospective test data.
The Architecture

A state-conditioned, segment-bounded Health Index.

Instead of black-box classification, the paper formulates a transparent, 5-component diagnostic score calibrated solely on healthy training operations.

Mathematical Formulation
$$HI(t) = \frac{1}{5} \sum_{i=1}^{5} \min\left\{1, \frac{\log(1 + a_i(t))}{\log(1 + q_{95,i})}\right\}$$

Alarm triggered when $HI(t) > Q_{99} = 0.644372$ persists for $P = 3$ consecutive samples ($\sim 30\text{ s}$).

Sensitivity Analysis

P = 3 is a chosen operational point, not a universal optimum.

Event 4 Lead Time vs False-Warning Rate
Threshold Robustness: Warning lead time remains broadly stable across calibration thresholds from Q97.5 to Q99.5. Setting the threshold too restrictively at Q99.9 completely misses Event 4. Overly strict persistence at P = 10 also fails to detect the anomaly.
Sensor Component Ablations: Removing Oil_temperature drops Event 4 lead time down to ~10.83 h. Removing Motor_current rolling std spikes the false-warning rate from 0.459% up to ~3.406%. Both sensors are vital to stability.
Comparative Workload

A useful warning is about manageable workload, not just lead time.

Scope & Boundaries What this study does not show

Single Held-Out Prospective Failure: Event 4 represents one documented prospective failure event in one physical system. It does not prove generalization across every compressor model, fleet, or mechanical failure mode.
No Downtime or Cost Reduction Measured: While advance lead time of 15.84 hours was established, real-world reduction in maintenance repair hours, train delay fines, or component replacement cost was not experimentally quantified.
SHAP is Attribution, Not Physical Causality: TreeSHAP scores quantify the mathematical reliance of a fitted ensemble model on specific input features; they do not establish thermodynamic or physical causation of mechanical failure.
Health Index is Not Remaining Useful Life (RUL): The score assesses progressive operational abnormality relative to healthy quantiles; it does not output a continuous statistical prognostic curve of hours until mechanical seizure.
Workflow-Specific Optimality: The Health Index is demonstrated to be the most viable option within the evaluated workflow and dataset, not a universally optimal algorithm for all industrial time series.
